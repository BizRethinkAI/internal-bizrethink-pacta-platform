## Evidence method

Body hashes identify decoded UTF-8 strings extracted from TypeScript object
literals at the recorded commit. The extraction did not execute the application
or access a production environment. Full source-file hashes preserve the
metadata/field context; the source link and line point to that revision.

HTTP attempts record exact URL, final URL, UTC time, status, content type, size
and raw SHA-256. TLS verification was not disabled. A response hash identifies
bytes retrieved, not legal authenticity, currency or completeness. The three
new retained PDFs are unmodified publisher responses; other retained extracts
have their own hashes and method labels. Most raw HTML responses are not
archived. Navigation and access failures remain in the attempt index.

The ten positive legal-span comparisons use NFKC, case folding and alphanumeric
characters, ignoring punctuation/whitespace. They cover the named prior spans
for CA Finance Code, VA code/rules, Missouri and six Florida sections; they are
not raw-byte comparisons or an independent search for additional law. Reused
files are hashed without editing the historical reports. The full source URLs
remain in the index and manifest so a later session can repeat a targeted check.

PDF text uses `pdftotext -layout`; the FTC conversion emitted font-weight
warnings. Its retained PDF controls, and no layout/conspicuousness approval is
inferred from extracted text. Web-tool evidence is labeled by observed date and
window; it has no invented raw-response hash. Source reading, rendered-form
verification, legal approval and operating compliance remain separate.
