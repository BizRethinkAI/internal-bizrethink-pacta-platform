Preserve enforceable terms in the executed package with stable references and review history; determine their final catalogue and numbering in the later design task. ## MCA-R02 — Funding fields conflict with the bargain and omit required bindings

**confirmed source inconsistency · high · New correction and data-contract task**

The 30-field grid still labels deferred equipment as added to Payback, although the operative terms exclude equipment from the Purchased Amount. Its Total Cost of Financing label defines the figure as Purchased Amount minus Net Amount Funded. Cash disbursed to the merchant is not a universal statutory finance-charge base. For example, with $100,000 provided, $140,000 purchased, a $30,000 third-party principal payoff and no fees, cash is $70,000; subtracting cash produces $70,000 rather than the $40,000 purchase discount. Utah separately defines funds provided and funds disbursed; California includes amounts provided on the recipient’s behalf in its amount-financed definition. This is a field-definition finding, not proof of a wrong live calculation. Buyer and reconciliation contacts, funding conditions/deadline, offer expiry, exhibits and broker compensation are referenced as grid entries but are not fully represented by these fields. Every field is marked required, including DBA; only one processor entry is modeled.

**Next action:** Define one typed funding/itemization contract with distinct provided funds, cash, prior principal, unpaid prior charges, fees, equipment election and state-specific calculations. Inventory every referenced blank and required/optional rule. Retain placeholders until actual transaction data arrives; do not bake one merchant’s numbers or undefined formulas into a provider template.

## MCA-R05 — Core MCA protections depend on servicing and on the whole guaranty

**operational prerequisite and drafting judgment · high · Preserve protections; validate each supported guaranty path**

The current FRPA allocates non-generation risk to Buyer, imposes mandatory reconciliation, avoids a finite collection term and does not accelerate ungenerated receipts for ordinary business failure or bankruptcy. Preserve that coordinated design. Full-performance is a materially broader guaranty than personally committed/directed misconduct even though both exclude a guaranteed Purchased Amount. The limited-conduct clause’s overlapping diversion limbs also deserve precise review when that option is finalized. No label or three-factor checklist is a legal safe harbor. Principis upheld a purchase on its record; Bridge’s majority treated its transaction as a purchase but reversed on damages proof; Kapitus left characterization for trial; Richmond affirmed liability where actual conduct defeated purported reconciliation and non-recourse protections. Richmond’s monetary award was vacated for recalculation, not wholly affirmed.

**Next action:** Record the provider’s actual reconciliation, refund, stop-collection, allocation and complaint capability. At transaction time retain processor acceptance and correct ledgers; later verify performance. Review any broader guaranty with the default/remedy bundle and relevant law before enabling it. Do not convert monthly reconciliation/refund deadlines into dispensable interview explanation.
