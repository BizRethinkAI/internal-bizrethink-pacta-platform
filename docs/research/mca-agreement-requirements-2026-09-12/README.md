# MCA agreement requirements — eleven-state source walk

**Georgia, Kansas, Missouri and Utah expressly require variable-payment
methodology in the financing agreement. Texas also requires a prescribed OCCC
notice and agreement support for charges.** Those duties defeat the old
“Texas only” assumption. They do not make every clause in the library legally
required, or make our wording prescribed.

This report supplies the dedicated commercial-financing-law review needed
before the clause-by-clause [ADR 0014](../../adr/0014-two-questions-every-clause-answers.md)
backfill. It covers the eleven states already represented by the MCA source
collection. The [section register](section-register.md) records the complete
statutory schemes and the California, New York, Texas and Virginia implementing
chapters read, including sections that regulate conduct instead of agreement
text. The [manifest](manifest.json) identifies retrieved sources and retained
evidence; the [source ledger](source-ledger.md) makes those records browsable.

Research date: **September 12, 2026**. Initial base: `01e53dcca`; integrated
main: `7f5ce5684`, including merged #176's CT/FL/MO corrections. The earlier
[source audit](../mca-source-audit-2026-09-12/README.md) remains an unchanged
historical record. This report adds to it; it does not rewrite its findings as
though the later checks had already happened.

## What the review establishes

The findings below are drafting analysis, not quotations unless marked as such.
“Agreement content” means the authority requires information or a contractual
arrangement. “Prescribed text” means it supplies words to reproduce. “Conduct”
includes timing, signatures, registration, records and enforcement. “Restriction”
means a term or practice is prohibited or constrained. One provision can do more
than one of these things.

## Consequences for ADR 0014

1. **Start with positive duties and restrictions.** Review the TX OCCC notice,
   variable-payment/reconciliation provisions, prepayment references, fees,
   security/ACH language, venue/arbitration, waiver provisions and assignment
   against the precise paragraphs above. Preserve the distinction between a
   clause that implements a duty and a verbatim prescribed notice.
2. **Use `compelled` only for text the authority requires to appear.** The
   Texas notice is a clear candidate. GA/KS/MO/UT prescribe information, with
   wording left to the drafter: the implementing agreement language is a
   candidate for `implements`, with state-specific applicability. These are
   candidate mappings; this report does not assign metadata to any record.
3. **A prohibited term is not automatically a required savings clause.** A rule
   limiting collection or venue can explain why a choice is fixed without
   prescribing our exact protective sentence. Assess `WhyThisClause` and
   `ClauseVariance` separately, as the ADR requires.
4. **Do not infer `discretionary` from silence in these acts.** The remaining
   clause-level review must consider the clause's actual function and any
   relevant UCC, contract, guaranty, bankruptcy, electronic-signature, ACH or
   case-law authority. Definition-only citations do not establish a statutory
   drafting duty. Conditional true-up definitions do not establish a judicial
   safe harbor against loan recharacterization.
5. **Keep prescribed disclosures on their existing surface.** CA/NY/VA
   separate-form requirements and TX/CT disclosure duties must not become
   optional agreement clauses. ADR 0008 and ADR 0014 expressly exclude
   `mca/content/` and `mca/prescribed/` from this metadata model.
6. **Backfill the current 211 records**, with reasons specific enough to review.
   Keep `status: 'draft'`, `author: null`, the fact-partition rule and the owner's
   commercial-option decisions. The ADR's 219 is a historical count. A complete
   source walk is input to the backfill, not approval of the corpus.

## Currency and access findings

- **California:** all 26 consolidated sections were read. The OAL-designated
  publisher states currency through **August 28, 2026, Register 2026 No. 35**;
  those sections show the 2022 adoption history. Current Division 9.5 and
  [2025 SB 362](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB362)
  were read separately. Effective January 1, 2026, current §22806 regulates
  pricing communications and §22807 supplies enforcement provisions; the
  estimated-APR protection is now §22805. The old rules PDF alone cannot supply
  those statutory updates. §953(b)'s published cross-reference is preserved,
  not editorially repaired. No claim is made to a September 12 consolidated
  CCR edition that the publisher did not provide.
- **New York:** all 26 Part 600 sections and FIS §§801–812 were read. The free
  NYCRR publisher expressly says **October 31, 2023** and is an unofficial
  compilation. DFS still links its official 2023 adopted PDF, but its final-
  adoption index's own update date is November 1, 2023; an updated emergency
  index is not proof of updated final rules. The official Register's full-year
  2023, 2024 and 2025 indexes, first-half 2026 index, and every weekly issue
  July 1–September 9, 2026 were checked for DFS rulemaking. No later Part 600
  amendment was identified. This is a documented amendment search, not a claim
  that Westlaw's printed currency date changed. Statute pages required web-tool
  or browser access after direct HTTP requests returned 403.
- **Connecticut:** the current DOB index links the August 1, 2024 guidance
  retrieved here. The October 17, 2024 no-action memo addresses bona fide
  employees; it is not an extension of the expired disclosure grace period.
  Current §§36a-868/870/872 use the 2026 supplement. The cross-referenced
  enforcement §§36a-50/51/52 were read, using the supplement for §51. Old
  guidance does not override the later registration statute.
- **Texas:** the complete 14-rule OCCC adoption packet and all 14 current SOS
  portal rules were read, beyond the four republished rules already vendored.
  Each portal rule gives July 9, 2026 / 51 TexReg 4363. The portal labels the
  second data-breach item in §86.311(i) `(2)`; the packet prints `(B)`. This
  publication discrepancy is recorded, not silently normalized into a claimed
  byte-for-byte match. The portal's chapter-review date of August 19, 2022 is
  not the effective date of these 2026 rules.
- **Georgia:** the legislature links to public Lexis Georgia Code. Its search
  result confirmed §10-1-393.18 and the 2023 enactment history; opening the full
  section encountered a CAPTCHA. The official enacted text is retained from
  #174. The legislature's 2024/2025/2026 general-statute summaries were searched,
  and their complete Title 10 sections read; no amendment to §393.18 was
  identified there. **A full current-code body comparison remains unclosed.**
  Legislative summaries and a search-result history are corroboration, not a

- **Utah:** the current regulator index links a FAQ whose effective marker is

- **Virginia:** the full current 10VAC5-240 chapter has four substantive
  sections, all with October 1, 2022 histories. The linked official form is

- **Florida, Kansas, Louisiana, Missouri:** use #174's complete current-code

## Evidence method and boundaries

Each new HTTP retrieval records the requested URL, redirect/final URL, UTC time,
status, content type, response size and SHA-256. HTTPS verification stayed
enabled. Raw-response hashes identify the bytes inspected; most raw responses
are **not** archived here. The manifest separately identifies the retained text
or PDF and its hash, and records unsuccessful routes. Browser observations have
their own ledger; they are not presented as hashed HTTP downloads.

California/New York sections are retained as complete legal bodies, with
source URLs and publisher version labels. PDF text was extracted with
`pdftotext`; Texas's two-column adopted-rule text additionally uses left-column
then right-column extraction, with the complete PDF retained as the authority.
Texas page 19 and the New York 2023 index's page 39 were visually checked to
confirm the notice and the amendment-index row. New York index excerpts identify
their source issue and selection. Some DOS responses contain a 422-byte PDF
generator-warning prefix before `%PDF`; raw hashes include that prefix.

This is a complete section walk of the **listed dedicated statutory schemes
and implementing chapters**, subject to the stated source-version limits. It
is not a fifty-state survey or an exhaustive review of every potentially
applicable law in eleven jurisdictions. It does not establish the legality of
full recourse, every arbitration option, a particular ACH arrangement, or any
individual transaction. Those questions receive targeted authority checks when
the relevant clause is backfilled or authored.
